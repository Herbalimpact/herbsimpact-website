If you want to prevent non-communicable diseases, start by understanding where most of them begin. There are many non-communicable diseases (NCDs), but the best known include the ones listed below.

![A health worker measuring a patient's blood pressure to help prevent non-communicable diseases](/assets/uploads/prevent-non-communicable-diseases-blood-pressure-check.jpg "Regular check-ups help catch circulation problems early. Photo: Ninthgrid / Pexels")

## The Most Common Non-Communicable Diseases

1. Diseases of the blood circulation system (heart disease, high blood pressure and stroke);
2. Various types of cancer;
3. Diabetes (especially the adult-onset type);
4. Kidney diseases;
5. Some diseases of the respiratory system, for example asthma;
6. Diseases of the nervous system (neurological disorders);
7. Diseases of the muscles and bones (musculoskeletal disorders);
8. Mental illnesses;
9. Some liver diseases; and
10. Diseases that affect the blood cells (for example sickle cell disease).

## The World's Leading Killers

Non-communicable diseases are the leading cause of death worldwide. According to the World Health Organization (WHO), these diseases kill an average of 41 million people around the world every year.

## The Root of Most NCDs: Poor Blood Circulation

The origin of most non-communicable diseases is poor functioning of the blood circulation system.

The main job of blood circulation is to deliver oxygen and various nutrients to the cells of the body, and to carry away the waste produced by the cells so that it can be removed from the body.

Many systemic diseases begin when these tasks are no longer carried out efficiently.

Do your calves, thighs or buttocks ache when you walk, then ease after a few minutes of rest? Many people blame age or tired muscles. But this pattern can be a sign that the arteries in your legs are narrowing.

The condition is called **peripheral artery disease (PAD)**. The surprising news is that one of its most effective treatments is free, simple and backed by strong research: **walking, done the right way.** In this article I explain the warning signs, who is at risk, and the exercise method that doctors recommend.

## What Is Leg Artery Blockage?

Arteries carry oxygen-rich blood from the heart to the rest of the body. Over the years, cholesterol and damage from smoking, high blood sugar and high blood pressure can build fatty plaque inside the artery walls. This is the same process that causes heart attacks and strokes. When it happens in the legs, the arteries narrow and less blood reaches the muscles.

At rest, the muscles usually get enough. But when you walk, they need more, and the narrowed arteries can't keep up. The result is the classic symptom: **pain, cramping or tiredness in the legs that comes on with walking and goes away with rest.** Doctors call it *intermittent claudication*.

PAD is far more common than most people think. It affects more than 200 million people worldwide, including about 10–15% of people over 50.

## Who Is at Risk?

The main risk factors are:

- **Smoking**, now or in the past (the strongest risk factor)
- **Diabetes**
- **High blood pressure**
- **High cholesterol**
- Age over 50, kidney disease, and heart disease or a past stroke

People with PAD also have a higher risk of heart attack and stroke, because the same narrowing is usually happening elsewhere in the body. That is why it is worth catching early.

## The Exercise Doctors Recommend: Walk–Rest–Walk

The 2024 guideline from the American College of Cardiology and American Heart Association recommends **structured exercise**, either supervised or as a structured community or home-based programme, to improve walking, daily function and quality of life in people with PAD.

The method is simple:

1. **Warm up** with 5 minutes of easy walking.
2. **Walk briskly** until the leg discomfort becomes **moderate**.
3. **Stop and rest** until the discomfort goes away (usually 1–5 minutes).
4. **Walk again**, and repeat the walk–rest cycles.
5. **Build up** from about 15 minutes of walking per session to 45–50 minutes, **three times a week for at least 12 weeks**.

Why walk into discomfort? In the LITE trial (*JAMA*, 2021), 305 people with PAD walked at home for a year. Those who walked at a pace that brought on leg symptoms improved their 6-minute walking distance. Those who walked at a comfortable, pain-free pace did no better than people who did not exercise at all. The discomfort is a sign the muscles are working, and it usually lessens as the weeks go by.

## Simple Leg Exercises for Rest Days

On two non-walking days a week, these exercises strengthen the muscles that carry you:

- **Heel raises:** holding a chair, rise onto your toes and lower slowly. 2–3 sets of 10–15. Your calf muscles act like a "second heart" for circulation.
- **Sit-to-stands:** stand up from a firm chair without using your hands, then sit down slowly. 2–3 sets of 8–10.
- **Ankle pumps:** point your toes away, then pull them towards you, 20–30 times. Useful during long periods of sitting.

If walking is too painful, cycling, climbing stairs or arm-cycling exercise are good alternatives. In trials, arm exercise also improved walking ability in people with PAD.

## Habits That Protect Your Arteries

Exercise improves how far you can walk. These habits protect the arteries themselves:

- **Stop smoking completely.** It is the most important single step.
- **Control blood sugar, blood pressure and cholesterol,** and keep taking any medicines your doctor prescribes.
- **Eat for healthy arteries:** plenty of vegetables, beans and whole grains, oily fish twice a week, and less salt, fried food and sugar.
- **Check your feet every day,** especially if you have diabetes. Small wounds heal slowly when blood flow is reduced.

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

See a doctor soon if you have leg pain when walking that eases with rest. Ask about the **ankle-brachial index (ABI)**, a quick, painless test that compares blood pressure at the ankle and the arm.

**Seek urgent care** for foot or toe pain at rest (often worse at night), a foot wound that won't heal, or dark or black toes. **Go to emergency care at once** if a leg suddenly becomes cold, pale, painful, numb or weak.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can exercise unblock leg arteries?** No. Exercise does not remove plaque that is already there. But structured walking improves how far you can walk, helps the muscles use oxygen better, and is linked to the growth of small side vessels around narrowed areas.

**Is it safe to walk when my legs hurt?** For people with PAD, walking until the discomfort is moderate and then resting is the recommended approach. Stop at once and seek help if you get chest pain, severe breathlessness or dizziness. Talk to your doctor before starting.

**How long before I see results?** Most people notice improvement after 4 to 8 weeks of three sessions a week, and the full programme runs for at least 12 weeks.

**What is the best test for leg artery disease?** The ankle-brachial index (ABI) is a simple first test. It compares the blood pressure in your ankle and your arm.

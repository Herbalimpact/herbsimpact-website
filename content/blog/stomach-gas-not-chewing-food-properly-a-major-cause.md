When gas builds up in the intestines, stomach gas is not only a nuisance; at times it can even cause pain in other parts of the body, or even threaten life.

![A smiling young man holding a bowl of food, a reminder that chewing well helps prevent stomach gas](/assets/uploads/stomach-gas-chew-food-slowly-gesi-tumboni.jpg "Eating slowly and chewing well gives your stomach a head start. Photo: Mikhail Nilov / Pexels")

There are several causes of gas in the body.

Today we will look at one of them.

## Why Not Chewing Well Causes Stomach Gas

The cause we will talk about today is not chewing food until it is soft before swallowing it.

Swallowing food that has not been softened well can leave the stomach unable to complete the work of digestion efficiently.

When this happens, pieces of food that were not digested in the stomach travel on to the large intestine.

Once they reach the large intestine, the bacteria that live there are forced to finish the job by fermentation.

Among other things, this fermentation produces a large amount of gas as a by-product.

## How to Prevent It: Eat Slowly and Chew Well

To avoid this, let us stop eating in a hurry, and make sure we chew our food until it is soft before we swallow it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What causes stomach gas?** Common causes include swallowed air, foods such as beans, cabbage and fizzy drinks, and undigested food fermenting in the large intestine. Eating fast and not chewing well is one of the most common causes, and one of the easiest to fix.

**How long should I chew my food?** There is no magic number. Chew each mouthful until it is soft and evenly mashed before you swallow, put your spoon or fork down between bites, and give each meal enough time.

**Does eating slowly reduce bloating?** For many people, yes. Eating slowly means you swallow less air and send better-prepared food to the stomach, so less undigested food reaches the large intestine to ferment.

**When is stomach gas a reason to see a doctor?** See a doctor if gas comes with severe or lasting pain, a hard swollen belly, vomiting, blood in the stool, unexplained weight loss or a lasting change in bowel habits. Pregnant or breastfeeding women and children should ask a health practitioner before using any remedy.

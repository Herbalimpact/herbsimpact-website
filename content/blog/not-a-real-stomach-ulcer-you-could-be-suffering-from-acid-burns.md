Some people experience every symptom associated with stomach ulcers, but what they're actually dealing with is something different entirely — acid burns, injuries caused by excess acidity affecting the walls of the small intestine.

## Why This Happens

This develops when the body becomes overloaded with acidity from diet, stress, or the pressures of everyday life. When the body's acid load gets too high, nearly every fluid in the body (aside from blood) shifts and takes on an acidic character.

## How the Body Normally Protects Itself

Fluids released from the pancreas and liver — bile and pancreatic secretions — are supposed to be alkaline, because their job is to neutralize leftover acid in digested food as it moves from the stomach into the intestine. If these fluids have also turned acidic, they add more acid on top of what's already there instead of neutralizing it.

## How the Burns Form

Digested food that hasn't been properly neutralized then burns the intestinal tissue as it passes through — creating injuries that look and feel like ulcers, but aren't caused by the same mechanism.

## Why Medication Alone Won't Fix It

Acid-burn injuries like these can't be treated with medication. The only real fix is correcting the diet — starting with drinking enough water relative to body weight (roughly one litre for every 25kg per day).

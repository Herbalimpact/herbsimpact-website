Carrying home a bag of fresh salad greens is supposed to be one of the easiest ways to eat healthy. But a fast-moving cyclospora outbreak linked to lettuce and bagged salad has sickened thousands of people across the United States in recent months, turning a simple side dish into a source of serious illness for some families.

## What Is Cyclosporiasis?

- **The parasite**: Cyclospora cayetanensis is a microscopic parasite that contaminates soil, water, or produce and causes a prolonged intestinal infection.
- **How it spreads**: unlike typical food poisoning, cyclospora is not spread person-to-person; it needs time in the environment to become infectious, which is why outbreaks are often traced back to imported or unwashed produce.
- **Recognized symptoms**: watery diarrhea, loss of appetite, weight loss, cramping, bloating, fatigue, and a low-grade fever that can drag on for weeks if untreated.

## Why This Outbreak Matters

- Investigators have linked the current outbreak to iceberg lettuce and bagged salad mixes across several states, with thousands of confirmed cases and a small number of deaths among people with underlying health conditions.
- Symptoms can take one to two weeks to appear after exposure, making it difficult to pinpoint the source without careful tracing.
- Because the parasite resists many common sanitizers, thorough washing does not always eliminate the risk once produce is already contaminated.

## Protecting Yourself and Your Family

- **Buy wisely**: choose produce from trusted, traceable sources and check for recall notices before eating bagged salads.
- **Wash thoroughly**: rinse leafy greens under running water, though remember this reduces rather than eliminates risk during an active outbreak.
- **Watch for symptoms**: watery diarrhea lasting more than a few days warrants medical attention and testing.
- **Support your gut naturally**: staying hydrated, easing back into bland foods, and sipping traditional soothing herbal teas such as ginger or peppermint can help ease digestive discomfort while your body recovers.

## When to See a Doctor

Seek medical care if diarrhea lasts longer than a few days, if you notice blood, or if you become dehydrated. Cyclosporiasis is treatable with prescription antibiotics, but it is frequently missed without specific lab testing, so mention any recent salad or produce consumption to your doctor.

## Bringing It All Together

Fresh produce remains one of the healthiest choices you can make, but outbreaks like this year's cyclospora surge are a reminder to stay informed about recalls, wash your food carefully, and listen to your body when something feels off. A little caution today can spare you weeks of digestive misery tomorrow.
